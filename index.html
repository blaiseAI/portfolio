I build small.
On purpose.

Five shipped things: a notebook, a hymnal, a kids' app, a prayer notch, a map of a continent's harvest. Not a startup. Just things that were needed.

I'm Blaise. I grew up Seventh‑day Adventist, which teaches you to notice small, overlooked needs — a hymnal nobody had digitized, a prayer habit that kept slipping through the day.

So that's what I build. Quiet, specific software for people I actually know: my own family, my own church community, my own prayer life, kids who like Bible stories before bed.

Five are shipped right now, across the web, iOS, and native macOS.

None of these were built to become a company. Each solved one actual problem for one actual person — so each looks different, because each need was different.

No account most people don't need. No dashboard to check daily. Each does one small thing, reliably, and gets out of the way.

Family Hub

A shared notebook for a household

Todos, shopping, notes, meals, bills and a house log in one place instead of six notebooks and a drawer. One capture bar on every screen reads plain sentences: “Nursery $420 due 12 Aug” becomes a bill, “Milk 2 × 2L” goes on the shopping list. Everyone in the household sees the same notebook, with who ticked what.

SDA Kinyarwanda Hymnal

Indirimbo z’Abadiventisiti, on iOS, Android and the web

The complete Seventh‑day Adventist hymnal, all 500 hymns in Kinyarwanda, for people who used to carry the hard copy to church. Search by number, title or a line of the lyrics, switch between Kinyarwanda and English, save favourites, and put a hymn up on screen with a slides mode for the service.

Selah

A prayer reminder that lives in the Mac’s notch

Every thirty minutes the notch opens to one line of Scripture and a Begin button. A gold arc follows your breath, then you say Amen and it folds away. No account, no reading plan, never steals key focus, and everything stays on the Mac.
